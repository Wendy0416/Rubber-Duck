About this project

This project starts from Walter Benjamin's essay "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction." Benjamin says that even a perfect copy is missing one thing, which is the original's "presence in time and space" (Benjamin 220). That unique history is what gives a work its aura, and it's what mass production takes away.

For this project, I've prepared five identical rubber ducks. Four of them are given different backstories, and the fifth only has a price tag. I want to see whether a story can give a copy its history back, and whether any of these ducks starts to feel like "the original."

"Even the most perfect reproduction of a work of art is lacking in one element: its presence in time and space, its unique existence at the place where it happens to be."Walter Benjamin, "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction"
